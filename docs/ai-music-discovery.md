# Verified AI music search

The iPhone/iPad AI Search screen uses the bridge's `POST /api/:client_id/music-discovery` endpoint. Deploy the matching bridge before installing the app. An older bridge produces an explicit update message; the app does not silently return to unverified suggestions.

## Search behaviour

- Every request is first routed to exactly one kind — chart, classical, albums or tracks — each with its own narrow parameters and its own resolver. All routes end in the same Roon verification. Tune a route inside its own section of the bridge's discovery instructions, then run the golden queries (see Verification).
- A UK singles chart request for one identifiable week, such as “UK top ten from this week in 1985”, is read from the published Official Charts page for that week, not from the model's memory. The interpreter receives today's date, so “this week in 1985” means the same day in 1985. Up to 40 positions are returned in chart order; entries Roon cannot verify stay listed as unavailable. A joint billing may match on its lead artist. A chart that cannot be loaded is an explicit error. Other charts, and whole years or decades, are answered as ordinary track suggestions.
- Track lookups search Roon for title plus artist before the title alone, because Roon lists songwriters as credits and a title-only search can surface a cover first. An unrequested live, karaoke, demo, remix or instrumental version is chosen only when no plain recording matches.
- Auto interprets the request; Tracks and Albums explicitly override the result type.
- Complete classical works such as Elgar's Enigma Variations return albums. A named movement such as Nimrod returns track recordings.
- A collection request such as “Brandenburg concerto” returns albums featuring the concertos. The number of works in a collection is never treated as its movement count, and collection matches do not claim verified completeness. Singular Concerto track headings match Concertos or Concerti searches. A specifically numbered concerto can be verified inside an album whose title names the whole collection; Play work only is offered for a verified single composition.
- AI supplies structured intent and candidate descriptions. Roon supplies the recording names, artwork, contents and playback paths. An unavailable suggestion has no active Play control.
- Album results contain the whole album plus a separately verified complete-work subset. Play album includes companion works. Play work only queues the selected work's sections in album order.
- All album searches look up release dates for the exact playable albums found in the Roon catalogue, including their performers and companion works. Matching dates include a source link. Ordinary album searches keep their catalogue order; latest searches sort new releases first, then reissues, then unknown dates. Missing or failed date research leaves the albums playable and explains the unverified dates. Latest searches explicitly say when results could not be ranked as the latest. This ranks verified catalogue results, not every release worldwide.
- All album searches, including latest searches, first use the UK Apple catalogue when album title and performer credits match. Full dates require matching track counts and consistent dates; a consistent year remains useful when editions differ or the catalogue gives a potentially year-only January 1 date. Conflicting years and future dates are rejected. Work/title queries cover multiple performers, supplemented by artist queries, with at most five requests and an eight-second timeout. These are release dates; recording dates remain separate when documented, and known reissues are labelled accordingly.
- Latest searches rank up to 80 lightweight Roon catalogue candidates by sourced dates before loading album contents. Verification uses at most three isolated browse sessions concurrently and stops once enough playable results are found, checking at most three times the requested result count (minimum 20 candidates). Completeness and performer checks still run for every returned recording, and playback always refreshes the selected album. Ordinary searches retain catalogue order.
- Web date research fills gaps only when needed, in at most four parallel groups of five albums. A latest search with enough dated catalogue matches proceeds immediately; otherwise optional web research has a 25-second deadline per group. Failed research preserves catalogue dates and successful groups. Exact album/performer matching, cited source URLs and full valid release dates remain required for web evidence; partial or future dates are rejected. Source titles may include artist credits and physical formats, but videos, different editions or a parent album cannot provide a separately titled audio release's date.
- The bridge logs interpretation, catalogue, date-lookup and verification timings plus candidate/result counts, without credentials. Optional research failures log a concise reason.
- Completeness requires the known movement/section count. If completeness cannot be established, the album is not labelled as a complete recording. Catalogue availability and release-date coverage can limit the results.
- While a search runs, the screen shows a turning record with expanding rings, the query being searched and a Cancel button; after 12 seconds it adds that classical and latest-release searches can take up to a minute. Reduce Motion shows a still record. Cancel discards the result and keeps the query for editing. The bridge finishes its own lookup regardless, and nothing is played.
- A lost network connection during search retries once after a short pause. Timeouts and server errors are returned without retrying. Playback is never retried after a connection loss, because the room's queue may already have changed.

## Playback

Results have server-generated IDs scoped to the paired client, expire after 30 minutes, and are lost on bridge restart. Playback refreshes and validates the saved Roon paths, metadata and ordered contents before modifying the selected room's queue. Roon browse item keys are not persisted. A changed or expired recording asks the user to search again.

Whole albums use Roon's album action; work-only and track selections use their verified track paths. Shuffle is disabled to preserve section order. Concurrent discovery play requests in the same room are rejected. The iOS screen waits for a fresh Roon playing event matching the selected music before navigating to Now Playing. Errors leave the search results visible.

Create Cinema preserves the verified recording paths and artwork for the chosen tracks or album.

The existing Siri and Apple TV search contracts remain supported. They receive the shared matching fixes, while the new typed discovery screen is an iPhone/iPad feature.

## Verification

Backend tests cover the original Petrenko album-title mismatch, reordered conductor/orchestra credits, wrong-performer rejection, complete versus excerpt selection, date provenance, expired/cross-client selections, catalogue changes and album/work queue actions. iOS tests cover decoding, unavailable selections and the album screen, details and mode control. Search and browsing themselves never invoke playback actions.

On 22 November 2026, the reported `Milos Karadaglic` / Albums request returned 10 verified albums (110 tracks) from the local bridge in 22.7 seconds. The screenshot reported a lost network connection; the original interruption was not reproduced. Added a single retry for that transport error on discovery searches only. All nine targeted discovery and recovery tests passed on the iPhone simulator, covering repeated failures, non-retryable errors, cancellation and no playback retry. The signed build was installed and launched on the connected iPhone Duo. Device inspection confirmed the search loading screen, but the phone returned to ChatGPT before final results could be observed; successful completion on the phone remains unverified.

The release-date follow-up was deployed to the local bridge on 22 November 2026. The same Albums request then completed with HTTP 200 in 46.8 seconds: all 10 albums remained playable, five had sourced full dates and three had sourced release years. Guitar Covers and Sour Times remained unverified. All 53 targeted discovery, routing, catalogue metadata and research tests passed, along with lint, TypeScript checking and the production build. The existing iOS date label supports both full dates and years, so this change needs no app reinstall. Live validation used a temporary paired client, removed it afterwards and issued no playback requests.

The `Brandenburg concerto` regression was reproduced on 22 November 2026: the original Auto request returned zero albums after 93.0 seconds even though Roon returned 45 album candidates for the literal search. Intent interpretation confused six concertos with six movements, and the work matcher missed singular Concerto headings. After separating collection intent from single-work completeness and matching concerto plurals, the deployed Albums endpoint returned 10 playable albums in 57.2 seconds, including Akademie für Alte Musik Berlin, Richard Egarr, John Butt and Claudio Abbado recordings. Auto and Albums interpretation both classified the request as a collection. All 61 targeted tests, lint and the production build passed; tests also cover numbered concertos inside collection albums, Tracks mode, and no misleading complete-work controls. Live checks used temporary paired clients, removed them afterwards and did not play music. No iOS reinstall was required.

The latest-search performance/date fix was deployed on 22 November 2026. A trace of the user's `Latest recordings of Elgar enigma variations` request had taken approximately 166.5 seconds: 5.7 seconds interpreting, 137.7 seconds browsing and validating albums, and 23.1 seconds researching dates, with no dates shown. The updated deployed Albums endpoint completed the exact query in 22.8 seconds with 10 playable complete-work recordings, all dated: seven full release dates and three verified years. It loaded 12 albums rather than fully validating the whole candidate pool. Each returned work contained 15 sections. All 67 targeted tests, lint, TypeScript checking and the production build passed, including ranking before content loading, bounded concurrency, unavailable-candidate fallback and preserving catalogue dates when web research fails. The diagnostic client was removed and no playback was invoked. No app reinstall was required.

The routing change was made on 24 November 2026 after `Find me the uk top ten from this week in 1985` stopped working: the discovery endpoint had no chart lookup, and the chart scraper itself was being refused (HTTP 403) because of its imitation-browser headers, so the older endpoint had been answering from model memory. The scraper now identifies itself plainly and returned the 100-position chart for 24 November 1985, headed by Wham!'s I'm Your Man. Eighteen golden queries covering all four routes run against the live model with `DISCOVERY_LIVE=1 yarn workspace @djehring/roon-web-api jest discovery-routing.live --coverage=false`. Repeated runs showed that the same query can yield different parameters at low reasoning effort (about 4 failures in 90 interpretations, 4.4 seconds each); `DISCOVERY_EFFORT=medium` reduced that to 1 in 90 at about 9.2 seconds each. The default remains low. All 632 bridge tests and TypeScript checking passed.

The change was deployed to the local bridge the same day. The first live run verified 8 of the 10 entries, and inspection of Roon's results exposed two matching faults. Roon credits songwriters beside performers, so a title-only search had selected covers that credit the original artist (Girls Aloud for See the Day, Céline Dion for The Power of Love); and joint chart billings such as Phil Collins and Marilyn Martin failed because Roon credits only the lead artist. Track lookups now search for title plus artist first, keep Roon's order, rank unrequested live, karaoke, demo, remix and instrumental versions last, and let chart entries fall back to the lead artist of a joint billing. The deployed endpoint then returned all 10 entries as the original recordings, in chart order, in 11.3 seconds. `Peace Piece by Bill Evans` (4.3 seconds), `Nimrod` (10 recordings, 65.2 seconds) and `Brandenburg concerto` (10 albums, 47.1 seconds) still returned correct results. Live checks used temporary clients, removed them afterwards and did not play music. No iOS change is required.

The local bridge was then switched to `DISCOVERY_EFFORT=medium` in `app/roon-web-api/.env` (the code default remains low). After the restart the chart query again returned all 10 originals in 13.7 seconds and `Peace Piece by Bill Evans` took 10.7 seconds, with interpretation taking 5.0 and 9.0 seconds respectively.

Interpretation speed was then compared across the models on the bridge's key, using the same golden queries (17 queries per round, timed per request). `DISCOVERY_MODEL` now selects the model, and the comparison is rerun with `DISCOVERY_COMPARE="model:effort,…" DISCOVERY_ROUNDS=n` on the live routing test. Over eight rounds, four combinations passed all 136 interpretations: `gpt-4.1` (median 1.0 seconds), `gpt-6-luna` at none (1.2), `gpt-5.4-mini` at none (1.3, 90th percentile 1.8) and `gpt-5.6-luna` at none (1.4). Over two rounds the existing `gpt-5-mini` took a median 3.8 seconds at low and 7.5 at medium. `gpt-5-mini` at minimal, `gpt-5.4-nano` and `gpt-4.1-mini` were as fast but made classical errors. The golden queries check routing and parameters, not the quality of suggested tracks for descriptive requests. The local bridge was then rebuilt and switched to `DISCOVERY_MODEL=gpt-5.4-mini` with `DISCOVERY_EFFORT=none` in `app/roon-web-api/.env`; the code default remains `gpt-5-mini` at low. The chart query returned the same 10 originals, `upbeat sixties soul for a dinner party` returned 10 verified tracks and `Latest recordings of Elgar enigma variations` returned 10 complete-work albums in 31.5 seconds. Interpretation took 3 to 4 seconds on the first requests after the restart and 1.1 to 1.6 seconds on the next three. No music was played.
